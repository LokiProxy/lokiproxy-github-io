## IP Type: The Biggest Dividing Line in Pricing


Products commonly referred to as "static IPs" on the market actually fall into three distinct categories:


### Data Center IPs

This is the most affordable tier, sourced from bulk IP blocks allocated by cloud service providers. Acquisition costs are extremely low, but all mainstream IP databases can easily identify their data center attributes.


### Rotating Pool "Pseudo-Static" IPs

These are essentially rotating IPs that providers "fix" to a user assignment. However, the IP may be reclaimed or replaced at any time, offering little stability assurance.


### Genuine Static Residential IPs

True [static residential proxies](https://www.lokiproxy.com/?utm_t=1&utm_i=52) refer to long-term fixed IP addresses assigned by legitimate ISPs to home broadband subscribers. These come with clear resource provenance and complete carrier registration records, offering significant advantages in sustained business operations and cross-regional backend management scenarios.


## Geographic Distribution: Scarcer Resources Command Higher Prices


Even within the same IP type, prices vary considerably across different countries and regions.

Regions such as North America and Europe benefit from well-developed internet infrastructure and abundant residential IP resources, making prices relatively affordable. In contrast, developed Asia-Pacific regions like Japan and Singapore have limited residential IP resources due to smaller geographic areas and dense populations, resulting in higher prices.


## Provider Cost Structure: Genuine Sourcing vs. Mixed Pools

Even when both are labeled "static residential IPs," the way providers acquire their resources directly impacts pricing. Reputable providers must establish partnerships with local ISPs and directly source home broadband resources, a process that typically involves multiple operational steps. Some lower-priced providers may cut costs by mixing IPs from various sources, but this often compromises IP purity and stability.
